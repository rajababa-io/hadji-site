Privacy policy

Last updated November 14, 2026

This policy explains what information Hadji collects, why, who it is shared with, and the choices you have. It covers the hadji.ai website, the early-access form, and the Hadji app at app.hadji.ai (together, the “Service”).

Hadji is run by Rajababa LLC, a Nevada limited liability company (“Hadji”, “we”, “us”). Questions about this policy go to ameet@rajababa.io.

1. Information we collect

Information you give us.

  • Early-access requests — the answers you type into our Google Form, such as your name, email, company and what you want to build.
  • Account details — when you sign in with Google: your email address, name and profile picture. You can also add a photo, time zone and display preferences.
  • Organization and project details — organization names and logos, members and invitations, and the projects and repositories you connect.
  • Your content — messages you send in sessions, files, pictures and videos you upload, issues and feedback, and the code, pull requests and other output the Service produces for you.
  • Keys and secrets — AI provider API keys or sign-ins, and secrets you store for your projects. These are encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and only placed into the private sandbox that runs your work.

Information from services you connect.

  • GitHub — when you install the Hadji GitHub App, we receive access to the repositories you choose, and store tokens needed to act on them.
  • Gmail (optional) — if you connect Gmail, Hadji gets read-only access so a session can read a thread you point it at. Tokens are stored encrypted. We do not send email from your account, and Gmail data is not used to train AI models or for advertising.
  • 1Password (optional) — if your organization connects a 1Password service account, Hadji reads only the items you reference to fill your secret records.
  • Other tools — anything else you connect (such as error trackers, databases or deploy targets) is accessed only to do the work you ask for.

Google API data. Hadji’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use Gmail data only to provide the feature you ask for: a session reading a thread you point it at. We send that data to the AI provider running the session only for that purpose. Gmail data is never used for advertising or to train generalized AI models. Humans do not read it except with your consent, for security, or as required by law.

Information collected automatically.

  • Usage and device data — pages and features used, browser and device type, IP address, and timestamps, collected through product analytics and logs.
  • Error reports — technical details when something breaks, so we can fix it.
  • Audit records — a record of important actions in your organization, including any time Hadji staff access an organization for support.

The hadji.ai marketing site itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics. It loads fonts from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address as part of that request.

2. How we use information

  • To provide the Service — run sessions, act on your repositories, store your history, and show your work back to you.
  • To keep it secure — sign-in, access control, fraud and abuse prevention, and audit records.
  • To support you — answer questions and investigate problems you report.
  • To improve it — understand which features are used and fix errors.
  • To contact you — about your early-access request, your account, or important changes.
  • To meet legal obligations.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not use your content to train our own AI models.

3. AI providers

Hadji sends the content of a session — your messages, relevant code, files and pictures — to the AI model provider that runs that session, so it can do the work. Depending on your settings this can be Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or another provider you choose (such as OpenRouter or Groq). When you use your own key or subscription, your agreement with that provider also applies to how they handle the data.

When the video skill is on, the sound of an uploaded video is sent to OpenAI to turn speech into text, and stills go to the AI provider running the session. A public YouTube link you share is sent to Google, and a rented browser from Browser Use may open the public video to take a screenshot. An organization admin can turn the skill off in Settings.

4. Who we share information with

We share information only with service providers that help us run the Service, under contracts that limit their use of it:

  • Hosting and infrastructure — Vercel (app hosting), Supabase (database), E2B (private sandboxes that run your work), Cloudflare (file storage, backups and DNS), Fly.io (network services), FalkorDB (session memory).
  • AI providers — as described in section 3.
  • Communications — Resend (email), Google (sign-in, forms and fonts).
  • Analytics and monitoring — PostHog (product analytics), Sentry (error reports), Axiom (logs).
  • Code hosting — GitHub, to act on the repositories you connect.

Inside an organization, other members can see that organization’s projects, sessions and shared content. A picture or page you choose to share by link is visible to anyone with that link.

We may also disclose information if required by law, to protect the rights and safety of our users or the public, or as part of a merger, sale or reorganization of our business (in which case this policy will continue to apply).

5. Cookies and similar technology

The Hadji app uses a small number of first-party cookies that it needs to work: one to keep you signed in, one to remember the last organization you used, and one used while installing the GitHub App. Product analytics are stored in your browser’s local storage. We do not use advertising cookies.

6. How long we keep information

We keep account and organization information, and your content, for as long as your account is active or as needed to provide the Service. When you ask us to delete your account or an organization, we delete or de-identify the related information, except where we must keep it for legal, security or dispute reasons. Database backups are kept for about 30 days and then expire, so deleted information may remain in a backup for that period.

7. Security

We use encryption in transit, encryption at rest for keys and secrets, isolated sandboxes for each piece of work, access controls and audit records. No system is perfectly secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. If we learn of a breach that affects your personal information, we will tell you as required by law.

8. Your choices and rights

  • You can update your profile in the app’s Settings.
  • You can disconnect GitHub, Gmail, 1Password and other services at any time, from Hadji or from that service.
  • You can ask us to access, correct, export or delete your personal information by emailing ameet@rajababa.io. We will respond within the time the law requires.

Depending on where you live (for example California, the EU or the UK), you may have additional rights, such as to object to or restrict certain processing, or to complain to your local data protection authority. We will not treat you differently for using these rights. Our legal bases for processing, where those laws apply, are performing our contract with you, our legitimate interest in running and improving a secure Service, your consent (for optional connections such as Gmail), and legal obligations.

9. International transfers

Hadji is based in the United States, and our service providers process information in the United States and other countries. Where required, we rely on appropriate safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

10. Children

The Service is for businesses and is not directed to children under 16. We do not knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. We will change the date at the top, and for important changes we will tell you by email or in the app before they take effect.
